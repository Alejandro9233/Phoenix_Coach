# Fuelling — carbohydrate, fluid and race day

Per-workout numbers are COMPUTED, not written here: `backend/services/fueling.py`
stamps a fuel line on every run and ride past 90 minutes, in Python, after the
plan is generated. This file explains those numbers so the coach can talk about
them. It never sets them.

## Hourly carbohydrate targets

- Under 90 min: nothing needed beyond normal daily eating.
- 90-120 min: 45-60 g/h. Routine long-session fuelling.
- Over 120 min: 60-90 g/h. This is race-gut training territory.
- Sub-3 marathon shape sits at the top of that band, 75-90 g/h.

The old 30-60 g/h guidance is superseded for sessions over two hours. Trials
report tolerance up to 120 g/h with a 3% economy benefit, but that upper figure
is disputed and needs months of gut training — 60-90 g/h is the defensible
target and the one the app stamps.

## Why the blend matters more than the number

Above roughly 60 g/h a single glucose transporter (SGLT1) saturates. Past that
point the extra carbohydrate has to arrive as fructose, which crosses on GLUT5,
a separate route. A glucose-fructose product is therefore not a preference: it
is what makes 90 g/h absorbable at all. Taking 90 g/h of a single-source
glucose drink is the textbook way to produce GI distress and then blame the
stomach rather than the product.

## Train the gut where a mistake is cheap

Practise the hourly rate on the long RIDE first. Three hours in Z2 is a
low-consequence rehearsal: if the stomach rebels you soft-pedal home. Once the
rate holds on the bike, transfer the same products and the same schedule to the
long run, roughly from the halfway point of a marathon block. The gut adapts to
what it is asked to absorb, so this is trainable — but only by training it.

## Fluid

- 8-10 ml per kg of body weight per hour when weight is known; 500-750 ml/h otherwise.
- Heat, not distance, is what moves this number. Add on hot days, not on long ones.
- Drink to thirst as the floor. Overdrinking is a real risk, not a theoretical one.

## Race morning

- A rehearsed breakfast of at least 100 g carbohydrate, 3-4 hours before the gun.
- A gel 5-15 minutes before the start — close enough that it does not trigger a glucose dip.
- Then the practised hourly rate, from the first aid station, before thirst or hunger.
- Nothing new on race day. Not the gel brand, not the drink, not the breakfast.

## Carb loading

- Three days out, raise carbohydrate to 8-10 g per kg of body weight per day.
- Cut fibre over those days to avoid GI trouble on the start line.
- Familiar foods only. Loading is not the week to discover a new cuisine.
